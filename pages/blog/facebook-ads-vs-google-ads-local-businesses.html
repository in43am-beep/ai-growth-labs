Facebook Ads vs Google Ads for Local Businesses

April 12, 2026 · Advertising · 14 min read

When local business owners think about paid advertising, two platforms dominate the conversation: Facebook Ads and Google Ads. Both can drive significant results, but they work in fundamentally different ways and excel in different scenarios. Choosing the right platform — or the right mix of both — can mean the difference between a profitable ad campaign and wasted budget.

The Fundamental Difference

Google Ads captures existing demand. When someone searches "emergency plumber near me," they already need a plumber right now. Your Google Ad appears at the moment of highest intent. Facebook Ads creates new demand. Your ad appears in someone's social feed, interrupting their browsing to introduce your business before they even start searching.

This distinction matters enormously for how you structure campaigns, write ad copy, and measure success.

Google Ads: Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • High intent: People are actively searching for what you offer right now
  • Immediate results: Start generating leads within hours of launching
  • Measurable ROI: Clear path from click to conversion to revenue
  • Local targeting: Target by zip code, city, or radius around your business
  • Budget control: Set daily budgets and only pay when someone clicks

Cons:

  • Expensive clicks: Competitive keywords can cost $20-$50+ per click for legal and medical services
  • Limited reach: Only reaches people actively searching (not building awareness)
  • Learning curve: Requires keyword research, bid strategy, and ongoing optimization
  • Click fraud risk: Competitors or bots can waste your budget with invalid clicks

Facebook Ads: Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Advanced targeting: Target by demographics, interests, behaviors, life events, and lookalike audiences
  • Lower cost per impression: Build awareness at a fraction of Google's cost
  • Visual storytelling: Use images, videos, carousels, and stories to showcase your work
  • Retargeting power: Re-engage website visitors and email subscribers effectively
  • Brand building: Create awareness among people who don't know you yet

Cons:

  • Lower intent: Users aren't actively looking for your service when they see your ad
  • Ad fatigue: Audiences tire of seeing the same ads quickly, requiring frequent creative refreshes
  • Privacy changes: iOS updates have reduced targeting precision and attribution accuracy
  • Platform dependency: Algorithm changes can dramatically impact campaign performance overnight

When to Choose Google Ads

Google Ads is typically the better first choice when your service has high search volume (people actively search for it), you need leads immediately, your average customer value is high enough to justify $5-$50+ per click, you're in a service industry where customers search at the moment of need (emergency services, plumbing, HVAC, dental), and you want the most direct path from ad spend to measurable revenue.

When to Choose Facebook Ads

Facebook Ads is typically better when you need to build brand awareness in a new market, your service is visually compelling (before/after transformations, beautiful spaces, food), you're targeting a specific demographic (new homeowners, parents, retirees), your product or service is impulse-friendly or aspirational, and you want to retarget website visitors or email subscribers with specific offers.

The Best Strategy: Use Both

The most successful local businesses use Google Ads and Facebook Ads together in a coordinated strategy. Use Facebook to build awareness and familiarity with your brand. Then use Google Ads to capture the search demand that Facebook creates. Finally, use Facebook retargeting to re-engage Google Ads visitors who didn't convert on their first visit.

This full-funnel approach with proper analytics tracking typically delivers 30-50% better ROI than using either platform alone.

Budget Allocation Guide

  • New businesses (building awareness): 60% Facebook / 40% Google
  • Established businesses (lead generation): 40% Facebook / 60% Google
  • Service businesses (high-intent): 30% Facebook / 70% Google
  • E-commerce/retail: 50% Facebook / 50% Google
