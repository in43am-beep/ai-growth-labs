CRO Best Practices: Double Your Conversions

April 6, 2026 · CRO · 15 min read

Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO) is the practice of increasing the percentage of website visitors who take a desired action — whether that's filling out a form, calling your business, making a purchase, or booking an appointment. For local businesses, even small improvements in conversion rate can translate to significant revenue gains.

Consider this: if your website gets 1,000 visitors per month and converts at 2%, you get 20 leads. Improving that conversion rate to 4% doubles your leads to 40 — without spending an extra dollar on advertising or SEO. CRO is the most cost-effective way to grow revenue from your existing traffic.

The CRO Framework

1. Research and Analysis

Before changing anything, understand how users currently interact with your site. Use analytics to identify pages with high traffic but low conversions, heatmaps to see where users click and scroll, session recordings to watch actual user behavior, and user surveys to understand friction points and objections.

2. Hypothesis Formation

Based on your research, form specific, testable hypotheses. For example: "Adding customer testimonials above the fold on our service pages will increase form submissions by 15% because visitors need social proof before committing." Every test should have a clear hypothesis, expected outcome, and measurement plan.

3. A/B Testing

Test one variable at a time to isolate what drives improvement. Common elements to test include headlines and value propositions, CTA button text, color, and placement, page layout and content order, form length and field types, images and visual elements, pricing presentation, and social proof placement.

4. Implementation and Iteration

When a test wins, implement the winning version permanently and move on to testing the next element. CRO is an ongoing process, not a one-time project. The best-performing websites are constantly testing and optimizing.

High-Impact CRO Strategies

Landing Page Optimization

  • Match your headline to the ad or search query that brought visitors to the page
  • Lead with benefits, not features — tell visitors what they'll gain
  • Use a single, clear CTA per page — remove competing actions
  • Include specific social proof relevant to the service on that page
  • Minimize navigation options on landing pages to reduce exit points

Form Optimization

  • Reduce form fields to the absolute minimum (name, email, phone is usually enough)
  • Use multi-step forms for complex submissions — they convert 86% better than single-page forms
  • Add progress indicators so users know how much is left
  • Use smart defaults and autofill to reduce friction
  • Place forms above the fold and repeat them further down the page

Trust and Credibility

  • Display review ratings and testimonials prominently
  • Show trust badges, certifications, and guarantee seals
  • Include real team photos instead of stock images
  • Add a visible phone number for visitors who prefer to call
  • Display case studies with specific, measurable results

Speed Optimization

Every 1-second improvement in page load speed increases conversions by 7%. Optimize images, minimize code, use a CDN, and invest in quality hosting. Your website design should prioritize speed alongside aesthetics.

CRO Metrics to Track

  • Conversion rate by page: Identify your best and worst-performing pages
  • Conversion rate by traffic source: Different channels convert differently
  • Micro-conversions: Track smaller actions (email signups, PDF downloads) that lead to macro-conversions
  • Revenue per visitor: Total revenue divided by total visitors
  • Test velocity: Number of A/B tests run per month (aim for 2-4)

Common CRO Mistakes

  • Testing without traffic: You need 1,000+ visitors per variation for statistically significant results
  • Changing too many things at once: Test one variable at a time to know what caused the improvement
  • Ending tests too early: Run tests for at least 2 full weeks to account for day-of-week variations
  • Ignoring mobile: Test and optimize mobile and desktop experiences separately
  • Optimizing for the wrong metric: Focus on revenue-generating conversions, not vanity metrics
